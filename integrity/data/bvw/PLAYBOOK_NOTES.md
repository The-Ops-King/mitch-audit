# Barak Van Wyk Coaching: closing playbook notes

Companion to `playbook_closer.json` ("BVW Master Closing Doc (completed from won calls)"). The playbook follows Barak's official Master Closing Doc (`sources/closing_doc.md`) in its order and wording. Where the doc is blank, the checks come from what the won calls show and end with "(script blank: from won calls)". A few checks the doc does not have at all, but the calls support, end with "(added: not in script, from call evidence)".

## What the official script is missing

The doc is fully written from the intro through the transition. After that it is a template. Every gap:

1. **Pitch, high-level promise:** ends on the placeholder "[BARAK'S ACTUAL PROCESS]". The doc never says how Barak does it.
2. **Pillar 1:** title is "[INSERT BARAK'S ACTUAL FIRST COMPONENT]", and the body is only a stage direction ("Explain the first stage and connect it directly to what the prospect said.") plus one generic line about why the relationship is stuck.
3. **Pillar 2:** title is "[INSERT BARAK'S ACTUAL SECOND COMPONENT]". The body is a fill-in frame: "(their pain)", "(current reality)", "(their desired reality)".
4. **Pillar 3:** title is "[INSERT BARAK'S ACTUAL THIRD COMPONENT]". The body is generic ("tools to actually create a healthier relationship").
5. **Delivery:** "[INSERT BARAK'S ACTUAL OFFER DELIVERY]", followed by seven empty fields: Duration, Calls, 1:1 access, Couple vs individual participation, Messaging/support, Resources, Barak's involvement.
6. **Next steps:** "[INSERT EXACT ONBOARDING PROCESS]". No payment step, agreement form or booking step is described. The only concrete item is a bare booking link at the very end of the doc ("BOOKING LINK for paid clients first call with B").
7. **Investment:** "[PROGRAM/TIER]" and "$X". No program names, prices or payment plans, and no rule for choosing couples or individual.
8. **Objections:** only the first-objection pacing move (money off the table, then the yes and uncertain branches). After "Then handle financial objection" there is nothing: no money handling, no payment plan, no step-down, no "think about it", no partner or spouse objection, no fear or belief objection, no follow-up when they don't pay on the call.
9. **Close:** there is no section for taking payment or booking Barak live. Nothing says payment happens on the call.
10. **No program facts anywhere:** the doc never says 12 weeks, how many sessions, the 80% track record, the rebuild-or-separate-with-closure end state, or the $500 One Call Session.

Two other mismatches matter. The setter tells leads the call is with the "Client Success Manager" and anchors the price at "low-to-mid four figures", but the doc does not check what the prospect was told before the price. Second, the doc's recording disclosure is never used: none of the 14 calls says the call is recorded.

## Phase by phase

### C1 Intro: rapport and frame (8). Source: script, plus two added checks

> "My name's Makena, by the way, I help Barack with his onboarding application process, good to meet you." (recGhnRsvZefXBVSx)

> "Are you still good for around 45 minutes?" (recG83GHIMQ9gNUul)

The script supplies confidentiality, recording disclosure, the transparency ask and the opening question, and no won call does any of them. All 14 calls open with her name and role and then the 45-minute check (C1f). C1g, the money decision-maker check, was added because of the contrast set: in recJJyTCIcSpWhIZE the wife who handles money was not on the call, and the sale ended with a $100 deposit.

### C2 Discovery: understand what happened (10). Source: both

> "How long ago did the infidelity happen?" (recGhnRsvZefXBVSx)

> "And when did this all happen? Like when did you find out about the infidelity?" (recG83GHIMQ9gNUul)

The script's questions match what she asks on every call. On the won calls she asks for the goal before the story ("what is your goal right now, either with yourself or with your relationship?"). The doc asks "what's happened that's brought you here today?" instead. The playbook keeps the doc's wording.

### C3 Isolate the real problem (10). Source: script, plus the why from won calls

> "Yeah, so if you like the feeling that it gives you, why do you want to break this cycle?" (recCs5H4hJqOJtQip)

The hardest-part question and both branches come from the script. C3f, the why under the cheating, is added from the wins. To figure that out." recGhnRsvZefXBVSx). Pillar 1 of the pitch depends on this answer.

### C13 Barak's method: the three pillars (12). Source: won calls (titles and content blank), script (the connecting lines)

> "So, identifying why you cheat, trace the behavior back to its kind of emotional, developmental origins." (recCs5H4hJqOJtQip)

> "So, repairing the impulse, the attachment, the validation systems, and basically building new ones to make cheating very unnecessary and unattractive to you, because right now you're getting a high from it." (recCs5H4hJqOJtQip)

> "So the program is structured around, nervous system safety, clarity, emotional boundaries, personal authority, and then self-trust." (recGhnRsvZefXBVSx)

The pillar names are not Barak's own. They are the three steps Makena walks through on the wins: find the why, repair and rebuild, make it last. For the hurt partner she lists a different set (recGhnRsvZefXBVSx). Pillar 2 carries both versions. On the won couples call she pitched no steps at all. She framed the process around Barak challenging both partners to take accountability and finding a middle ground (C13g). Barak should confirm or rename the three pillars.

### C18 Objections (12, applies when an objection is raised). Source: script (pacing), won calls (everything after)

> "I just don't know that, like, you thinking about it is necessarily gonna give you the answers as much as talking to him will." (recCs5H4hJqOJtQip)

> "Are you open to doing the first four sessions with Barack and seeing how they go and then deciding from there if this is something that you want to move forward with?" (recGhnRsvZefXBVSx)

> "And then if you do end up deciding to continue, you can just use that towards the rest of the program" (recCs5H4hJqOJtQip)

> "Um, are you guys both open to doing, to starting with the four sessions" (recG83GHIMQ9gNUul)

All three wins are step-downs, and in each the doubt was about certainty, not cash:
- recGhnRsvZefXBVSx: fear that getting happier would drive her husband away.
- recCs5H4hJqOJtQip: "It's not the money, it's really not the money".
- recG83GHIMQ9gNUul: whether the partner would actually act ("can it be six weeks").

On the couples win, each partner gave their own yes before payment ("I'm up for that." / "Yeah, me too."), which is C18h. In the contrast set the step-down either never came (recwyYh5D2HTXy3oB, rec1tlTKpupDVqmfF) or came only after a long back-and-forth (recK8cDNTYByeQw69, recPLMk8RaNuavRQv). The reconnects were left open ("just send me a text", recwyYh5D2HTXy3oB), which is why C18i asks for a dated follow-up.
